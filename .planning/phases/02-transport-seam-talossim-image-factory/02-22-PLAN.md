<objective>
Close the budget half of G-02-1 and G-02-2: give the two upstream workloads that are not JSON
endpoints their own budgets, compose those budgets against the response budget explicitly, and make
the composition an assertion rather than an arithmetic nobody performed.

Purpose: `imagefactory.DefaultTimeout = 30s` is sized in its own doc comment against "the three JSON
endpoints this package calls — the largest answer is the extension catalog at a few tens of
kilobytes". The same constant governs `ProbeBuildable`'s HEAD of the ISO URL, which makes the Factory
build a ~335MB image synchronously and was measured cold five times across two investigators at
30.50, 30.59, 31.18, 31.52 and 32.69 seconds. Every one of those exceeds the budget, by one to three
seconds, every time, for any schematic the Factory has not built before. The verdict is not wrong; it
is absent.

`GET /api/v1/schematics/{id}/assets` has the paired failure from the other side: two installer
candidates, each bounded independently by the same 30s, for a worst case of exactly 60.000s against
`writeTimeout = 60s` — equal, not inside — measured as `status=502 duration=1m0.002907792s`, a
problem+json flushed to an already-expired socket. `POST /api/v1/schematics` is worse still at three
sequential calls.

The user ratified Option 2 of `02-DECISION-probe-budget.md` on 2026-09-03: compose the budgets, keep
create synchronous, add no route and no probe state. This plan is the first half of that — the
budgets themselves and the guard that composes them. Plan 02-23 takes the serial candidate walk and
plan 02-24 takes the 409 and the ledger.

The guard is the most important artifact here. `cmd/holzkube-managerd/budget_test.go` already exists,
already declares both Factory routes `knownOverBudget`, and already says in its own comment that a
row which becomes within budget must be flipped and its ledger entry closed. This is the plan that
comes back and flips them — and that has to widen the table's arithmetic first, because
`calls × DefaultTimeout` is only correct while there is one constant, and after this plan there are
three.

Output: three named budgets with three stated derivations, a package where no request reaches the
wire without a deadline, two routes that declare their own ceiling, a `writeTimeout` that covers
them, and a guard that recomputes all of it from the constants that actually run.

  <behavior>
    The guard's rule after this task is stated in full here, because it is the artifact of the round
    and an executor must not have to infer it. Five assertions per row, each with its own failing
    direction. `sum` is the total of the row's declared upstream call budgets; `maxCall` is the
    largest of them.

    - **R0, declaration.** A row with at least one declared upstream call must declare a non-zero
      `routeDeadline`; a row with none must declare a zero one. *Fails when* a route that talks
      upstream declares no ceiling, or a store-only route declares a ceiling it does not apply.
    - **R1, the response-budget composition.** `routeDeadline + budgetSlack < writeTimeout`,
      vacuously true at a zero deadline. *Fails when* a route could still be writing after the
      server has stopped listening — the `2 × 30 == 60` shape, now stated once.
    - **R2, no constant is a fiction.** `routeDeadline >= maxCall`, vacuously true with no calls.
      *Fails when* a route's ceiling is shorter than one of its own calls' budgets, which would make
      that call's constant a number that never applies.
    - **R3, the verdict ratchet.** The computed verdict is `withinBudget` exactly when R1 and R2 both
      hold, and `knownOverBudget` otherwise. It is compared against the declared one and *fails in
      both directions*: a row that becomes over budget goes red, and a row still declared over budget
      after it has been fixed goes red. `knownOverBudget` with an empty `deferredTo` fails;
      `withinBudget` with a non-empty one fails. This is the existing ratchet, unchanged in form, now
      ratcheting on R1 ∧ R2 rather than on the retired sum comparison.
    - **R4, the clipping ratchet.** The computed clipping is `clipped` exactly when
      `sum > routeDeadline`, and `uncut` otherwise. It is compared against the declared one and
      *fails in both directions*. `clipped` requires a non-empty declared rationale; `uncut` requires
      an empty one. Clipping is **not** a failure: it is a declared, argued property of a route whose
